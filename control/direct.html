Control · General verb · Direct

How do we steer bodies and living things?

To direct is to aim: a bicycle, a herd, a spacecraft, a germ, a gene. This page keeps to physical and biological steering. For persuading minds, see mind.curiosta.com.

Start here: riding a bicycle

Ride a bicycle in a straight line. You are not holding still: you make dozens of small steering corrections a minute without thinking. What are you correcting for, and how do you know?

Pause and answer
  1. What tells you that you are starting to lean?
  2. Which way do you steer to recover?
  3. How is steering a bicycle like steering a spacecraft to a landing?
Compare your reasoning

Your inner ear and eyes sense the lean, and you steer slightly into it so the wheels come back under you. You aim, measure the error, and correct. A lander on the Moon does the same with sensors, a computer and thrusters. Directing living things is harder still: a vaccine or a drug steers the body’s own systems towards health.

01

The everyday case

Steering is aim plus correction.

A cyclist steers into a lean. A shepherd moves a flock by standing where the flock should not go. A doctor gives a vaccine so the immune system learns a target. Each one aims a physical or living system that has its own motion.

02

The mechanisms

We direct living things through their own machinery.

  • Vaccines. Edward Jenner’s cowpox work began in 1796 (History of Vaccines).
  • Antibiotics. Penicillin was first given to a patient on 12 February 1941 in Oxford (University of Oxford).
  • Genes. Watson and Crick’s DNA structure was published on 25 April 1953 (Nature); a programmable CRISPR–Cas9 DNA cutter was published online on 28 June 2012 (Science).
  • Guidance. Spacecraft steer with sensors and thrusters; Hubble holds its aim within 0.007 arcseconds (NASA).

04

The record, and how far it goes

A disease that killed about 3 in 10 of those it infected, steered to zero.

Record: smallpox killed about 3 in 10 of those it infected; on 8 May 1980 the 33rd World Health Assembly declared it eradicated (CDC).

How far from normal?

“Normal” for most of history was smallpox as a constant threat. Directing a living system all the way to zero cases is about as far from normal as a biological record can go.

05

Limits

Germs evolve; vehicles drift; bodies vary.

  • Resistance. Bacteria evolve resistance to antibiotics.
  • Drift and delay. Every guidance system fights noise and delay.
  • Scope. Steering minds and choices is outside this page; see mind.curiosta.com.
02 · Distance from normal

How far the records go from normal

Direct: a disease steered to zero

On a normal day a human body steers itself through balance and immunity. People have pointed a telescope to within 0.007 arcseconds, landed on the Moon by computer, docked satellites, and steered one disease to zero cases worldwide.

03 · Review

Check your reasoning

Answer each one in your notebook before you open it.

Why do you steer into a lean on a bicycle?

Steering into it brings the wheels back under your centre of mass.

What made smallpox eradication possible?

Vaccination stopped the virus spreading between people until no cases were left; the World Health Assembly declared it eradicated on 8 May 1980.

Why is persuasion not on this page?

This site keeps Direct to physical and biological steering; persuasion and the mind are covered by mind.curiosta.com.
