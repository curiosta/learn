Control · General verb · Convert

How do we turn one energy into another?

Every machine is a converter: food into motion, coal into steam, motion into electricity, electricity into light and heat. Convert is the verb under all the others.

Start here: charging a phone

Plug in a phone. It gets slightly warm, and an hour later it holds enough energy to light a screen all day. Somewhere a power station burned fuel, spun a turbine or caught sunlight to make that possible. How many times did the energy change form before it reached your pocket?

Pause and answer
  1. Name every form the energy took, from the power station to the screen.
  2. Why does the charger get warm?
  3. Can any conversion be perfect?
Compare your reasoning

A typical chain: chemical energy in coal becomes heat, heat boils water, steam spins a turbine, the turbine spins a generator that makes electricity, the charger converts it to the low voltage your battery stores chemically, and the phone turns it back into light, sound and heat. Every step loses some energy as heat, which is why the charger is warm. No conversion is perfect; physics says the total energy is kept, but some of it always ends up as low-grade heat.

01

The everyday case, worked

Two everyday conversions you can check with a label and a watch.

Worked: a kettle as a converter

One litre from 25 to 100 °C needs about 314 kJ (see Heat). At an assumed 2 kW that is about 2.6 minutes of electricity turned into heat. Read your phone charger’s label (watts) and time a charge to estimate how much electrical energy your phone takes in, then compare it with the battery’s rated watt-hours.

Electric heating is the one conversion that is nearly 100% “efficient”, because heat is where every conversion ends up anyway. Turning heat back into motion or electricity is where the losses lie.

02

The mechanisms

A handful of tricks do almost all the converting in the world.

  • Induction. Michael Faraday showed on 29 August 1831 that a changing magnetic field drives a current in a wire (Britannica). Every generator since spins magnets and coils.
  • Chemical cells. Alessandro Volta described his pile, the first battery, in a letter dated 20 March 1800 (Britannica).
  • Heat engines. Burn fuel or split atoms, boil water, spin a turbine (see Boil).
  • Light into electricity. Bell Labs demonstrated the first practical silicon solar cell on 25 April 1954, at about 6% efficiency (APS).
  • Electricity into light. Theodore Maiman’s laser first worked on 16 May 1960 (HRL).

04

The record, and how far it goes

For the first time, a lab released more fusion energy than it put in.

Record: on 5 December 2022 the National Ignition Facility put 2.05 MJ of laser energy into a fuel capsule and got 3.15 MJ of fusion energy out (LLNL).

Worked: gain

3.15 ÷ 2.05 ≈ 1.54. Target gain above 1 is the line called ignition. The lasers themselves drew far more energy from the grid than 2.05 MJ, so the plant as a whole was not yet a net producer; how much more is TBC here.

05

Limits

You can convert energy, but never without a little turning into waste heat.

  • Heat engines are capped by the temperatures they run between, the same Carnot limit that caps fridges (see Cool).
  • Storage is the bottleneck. Sun and wind vary; the Charge page (planned) will cover batteries.
  • Bodies convert too. A resting human turns food into about 100 W of heat; see Normal on Earth.
02 · Distance from normal

How far the records go from normal

Convert: from sunlight’s 1,361 W/m² to fusion ignition

On a normal day the energy income at Earth is sunlight, about 1,361 W/m² above the air, and nature’s converters, plants, capture only a small share of it (figure TBC). People first beat a 1:1 target gain in fusion in 2022 (gain about 1.54), and heated matter to about 19 billion times normal temperature (see Heat).

03 · Review

Check your reasoning

Answer each one in your notebook before you open it.

Why is an electric heater close to 100% efficient while a power station is not?

All the electricity becomes heat, which is the heater’s job. A power station must turn heat into motion, and heat engines are capped by the temperatures they work between.

What did Faraday discover in 1831 that every generator uses?

That a changing magnetic field drives current in a wire: electromagnetic induction.

NIF’s gain was 1.54. Why is it not yet a power station?

The gain compares fusion energy with laser energy delivered to the target. The lasers drew much more from the grid, and the shot lasted a fraction of a second.
