Control map / Value quadrant / Onion case

Control · Worked case · Time and space

Why does an onion cost four times more in October?

Nothing about the onion changes. It waits, then it travels. A worked case in rupees and kilowatt-hours, from a mandi in Nashik district to a city shelf.

Start here: the same onion, two prices

In May a farmer near Lasalgaon sold onions for about ₹12 a kilo. In late October the same mandi quoted about ₹44. On the same day a shop in your town may have charged over ₹50. The onion is the same vegetable. What were you paying for?

Pause and answer
  1. Name two things that happened to the onion between May and the shelf.
  2. Which of them used energy, and which mostly used time and money?
  3. Why would anyone sell in May if October pays four times more?
Compare your reasoning

It was held (time) and carried (space). Carrying burns fuel or electricity; holding mostly ties up money and risks loss. Farmers sell in May because they need cash, lack storage, and cannot know that October’s price will be high. In other years prices fall instead. Holding is a bet, not a sure profit.

Where the value is

Where does an onion gain value between May and October?

We follow one kilogram of onion from Lasalgaon, Asia’s best-known onion mandi, through two levers: time (hold it from the cheap harvest season to the dear autumn) and space (carry it to where buyers are). Prices are dated. Every “our estimate” is our arithmetic on them. Value added at a step is the price after the step minus the price before it, net of losses.

Where this sits on the value quadrant: Time and space together. The onion gains most by being held (time) and then carried (space); its form barely changes. See the quadrant.

Working: how each estimate was made
  • Time step. ₹43.26 × (1 − 0.0727) ≈ ₹40.11 and ₹45.00 × 0.9273 ≈ ₹41.73. Minus the May price gives 40.11 − 13.50 ≈ 26.6 to 41.73 − 11.00 ≈ 30.7 rupees per kg. NABCONS’s 7.27% is a whole-chain post-harvest loss, used here as a stand-in for storage loss (TBC).
  • Space step. The all-India wholesale average (₹46.47) minus the Lasalgaon price gives ₹1.47–3.21/kg. Freight for 1,000 km: ₹3.6/tkm × 1,000 km ÷ 1,000 kg = ₹3.6/kg by road, ₹1.6/kg by rail. Energy: 2.03 MJ/tkm × 1,000 ÷ 3.6 ÷ 1,000 ≈ 0.56 kWh/kg by diesel truck, 0.352 × 1,000 ÷ 3,600 ≈ 0.10 kWh/kg by rail. Value per kWh: 1.47 ÷ 0.56 ≈ 2.6 to 3.21 ÷ 0.56 ≈ 5.7 by road; 1.47 ÷ 0.098 ≈ 15 to 3.21 ÷ 0.098 ≈ 33 by rail.
  • Caution. An all-India average is not one city’s price, the 1,000 km is our illustration, not a route, and the 2021 freight rates are older than the 2026 prices. The table shows the shape of the chain, not a trading plan.

What you can do with this

  • Small business: price the hold before you buy

    Row 2 is where the value is, but it ties up money for months. Bought at ₹13.50 and with a 7.27% loss, the break-even sale price is 13.50 ÷ 0.9273 ≈ ₹14.56/kg before storage, interest and labour. Add those, then ask how much working capital the hold needs, and what happens if prices fall instead.

  • Skill: choose the mode, then the margin

    Row 3 shows that a 1,000 km road haul (≈ ₹3.6/kg) can cost more than the price gap it captures (₹1.5–3.2/kg), while rail (≈ ₹1.6/kg) can fit inside it. Recompute the contribution margin per truckload or per rake for a real route. The mode table below gives the inputs.

  • Home habit: watch the season

    DoCA publishes daily retail prices for onion, tomato and potato. Note your centre’s price once a week for a season. You will see the time lever from the buyer’s side, and why tomato (row 5, a higher loss) behaves differently from onion.

01

The physics in plain words

A stored onion is alive. It breathes, sprouts and dries out, and every lost gram is lost money.

Harvested vegetables keep respiring: they burn their own sugars and give off water and heat. Storage slows this down. Potatoes in Indian cold stores are kept at about 4 °C and 80–95% relative humidity (Singh et al. 2014), using 9.8–16.2 kWh of electricity per tonne each season (Singh et al.). The onion store’s own conditions and energy use are TBC for this page. Losses still happen: a national study put post-harvest losses at 7.27% for onion, 5.96% for potato and 11.62% for tomato (NABCONS via PIB).

Carrying is ordinary mechanics. The energy needed to move a tonne one kilometre depends mostly on friction and drag, which is why a steel wheel on a steel rail, or a hull in water, needs far less than a rubber tyre on a road or a wing in the air.

Working: the drone figure

RAND’s baseline design needs about 1.5 kWh to deliver a 5 lb (2.27 kg) payload out to a 10-mile (16.1 km) radius. 2.27 kg × 16.1 km = 0.0365 tonne-km; 1.5 kWh ÷ 0.0365 ≈ 41 kWh ≈ 148 MJ per tonne-km, counted on the one-way distance. Energies 2022 MJ figures are converted from its per-100-tonne-km values. NITI and RITES costs are for different years, so compare within a column, not across sources.

Two takeaways

  • Energy per tonne-km spans about 1,500 times, from a ship (0.099 MJ) to a delivery drone (≈150 MJ, our estimate). Drones only pay where value per kilogram or speed is very high.
  • Cheapest in India is not road. Both cited studies price rail below road per tonne-km. Road wins on door-to-door speed and flexibility, which matter most for perishables.

04

The record spread, dated

The size of the time lever in 2026, measured at one mandi.

Record for this page: Lasalgaon onion prices rose from ₹1,100–1,350 per quintal in May 2026 (Financial Express, Acrop) to ₹4,326–4,500 per quintal on 27–29 October 2026 (Acrop, MandiPulse).

Worked: the ratio

4,326 ÷ 1,350 ≈ 3.2 and 4,500 ÷ 1,100 ≈ 4.1. The October price was about 3 to 4 times May’s. This is one season at one mandi, not a rule: in a glut year the gap can be small or negative.

05

Try it: track a spread

Measure the time lever yourself.

You need: a notebook or spreadsheet and the DoCA daily price page (DoCA).

  1. Each Friday, note the retail and wholesale price of onion, tomato and potato.
  2. Compute the retail-minus-wholesale gap for each.
  3. After eight weeks, which vegetable’s gap moved most? Which has the highest loss in the NABCONS figures?
06

Limits: what stops the arbitrage

A price gap is not a profit until every cost and risk is paid.

  • Loss. 7–12% of the produce can be lost after harvest (NABCONS).
  • Money. Holding ties up working capital for months; storage rent and interest are costs even when prices rise.
  • Risk. In a glut the later price can be lower. Holding is a bet.
  • Averages. All-India prices hide wide city-to-city differences. A real plan needs one route’s prices.
  • Rules. Trade in food can be subject to government stock and export rules; check the current ones (TBC).
02 · Distance from normal

How far the records go from normal

Onion case: almost no distance from normal

Normal is 288 K, one atmosphere. A truck carries onions at ambient conditions. An Indian potato cold store holds about 4 °C (277 K), only 11 K below normal, or 0.96 times it. The value in this chain comes from time and place, not from extreme conditions. That is why it suits small businesses: it needs little equipment, but it does need money and judgement.

03 · Review

Check your reasoning

Answer each one in your notebook before you open it.

Why can holding onions add more value than carrying them?

In 2026 the May-to-October price gap at Lasalgaon (≈ ₹27–31/kg after loss) was much bigger than the gap between Lasalgaon and the all-India wholesale price (≈ ₹1.5–3.2/kg).

A 1,000 km road haul costs about ₹3.6/kg. When does it pay?

Only when the destination price exceeds the origin price by more than the freight and losses. Rail at ≈ ₹1.6/kg fits inside a smaller gap.

Why does tomato behave differently?

It has a higher post-harvest loss (11.62%) and, for this page, an unconfirmed storage life, so it relies on the space lever more than time.

What is the break-even sale price for an onion bought at ₹13.50 with a 7.27% loss?

About ₹14.56/kg, before storage, interest and labour.
