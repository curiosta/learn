Control · Contain · Pressurise

Why must a CNG cylinder weigh five times the gas it carries?

Pressurising means holding a fluid above the pressure outside. The squeeze stores energy and changes what matter does; the container must resist it everywhere, all the time. From a cooker’s whistle to a diamond anvil at 9 million atmospheres, the wall is the story.

Start here: the cooker’s whistle

A pressure cooker on the stove hisses, then whistles. Lift it off too early and open it, and the steam can scald. A closed steel tank of compressed gas sits quietly under a car, yet it is thicker and heavier than the gas it holds.

Pause and answer
  1. What pushes the weight up on the cooker’s vent?
  2. Why does the cylinder wall need to be so thick?
  3. What would happen if the wall had one weak spot?
Compare your reasoning

Steam trapped inside raises the pressure until it can lift the weight on the vent; the whistle is the excess escaping. Pressure pushes equally on every square centimetre of the wall, so the wall must be strong everywhere: a 50-litre CNG cylinder weighs about 48 kg empty and carries about 9 kg of gas (PNGRB). 48 ÷ 9 ≈ 5.3. One weak spot becomes the point of failure.

Where the value is

Where does pressure pay per unit of energy?

Pressure changes form: it packs gas into a cylinder small enough for a car, and it forces nitrogen and hydrogen to become ammonia, the base of urea fertiliser. Below are the processes we could source. Only one row has both energy and price; it uses a government-fixed price, so read it with care.

Where this sits on the value quadrant: Time on the quadrant: a cylinder or a cabin holds matter at pressure until it is needed. Pressure also pulls the Form lever, our third lever, when Haber–Bosch turns nitrogen and hydrogen into ammonia; CNG adds Space, because compressed gas can travel in a vehicle. See the quadrant.

Working: how each estimate was made
  • Urea. Urea is made from ammonia and CO₂: 2 NH₃ + CO₂ → CO(NH₂)₂ + H₂O, so 1 kg of urea needs 2 × 17.03 ÷ 60.06 ≈ 0.567 kg of ammonia (from the formula). 27.4 GJ/t × 0.567 ≈ 15.5 MJ ≈ 4.3 kWh; 31.8 × 0.567 ≈ 18.0 MJ ≈ 5.0 kWh. Price: ₹242 ÷ 45 ≈ ₹5.38/kg. Per kWh: 5.38 ÷ 5.0 ≈ 1.07 to 5.38 ÷ 4.3 ≈ 1.24. The farm price is fixed by law and is below cost; the government pays the gap as subsidy (PIB). The energy is mostly natural gas feedstock and fuel, not electricity. So this is not a market value.
  • CNG. Ideal isothermal work per kg: (R ÷ M) × T × ln(p₂ ÷ p₁). Methane: R ÷ M = 8.314 ÷ 0.01604 ≈ 518 J/(kg·K) (NIST WebBook). From 1 atm to about 200 kg/cm² gauge (≈ 195 atm absolute, PNGRB) at 298 K (assumed): 518 × 298 × ln 195 ≈ 0.81 MJ ≈ 0.23 kWh. Real compressors need more; how much is TBC.

What you can do with this

  • Small business: know when a price is set by policy

    Row 1: urea’s price is a statutory MRP, and the gap to cost is paid as subsidy, ₹1,03,319.50 crore for indigenous urea in 2024-25 (PIB). A business whose gross margin rests on a fixed price depends on policy more than on the market. Check which of your inputs and outputs are regulated before you plan.

  • Skill: read a pressure gauge in three units

    Row 2: CNG is quoted in kg/cm², weather in hPa and tyres in psi. One kg/cm² is 98.0665 kPa, from standard gravity (NIST); one atmosphere is 101.325 kPa (NIST). Converting fluently is the first step to working with compressors, cylinders and boilers safely.

  • Home habit: time your cooker against an open pot

    Row 3: cook the same cup of dal once in a pressure cooker and once in an open pot, and note the minutes on the same flame. The cooker’s water boils at about 120 °C instead of 100 °C (see Boil). How much gas you save is TBC: measure it.

01

The physics in plain words

Pressure is a push on every square centimetre at once.

Pressure is force divided by area. The air presses on everything at sea level with 101,325 Pa, about 10 tonnes-force on each square metre (NIST). We feel nothing because our bodies push back equally. Pressurising means making the inside push harder than the outside; the wall must then carry the difference. Compressing a gas also takes energy, which is stored and can be released, sometimes violently.

Worked: kg/cm² to atmospheres

One kilogram-force is 9.80665 N (NIST), so 1 kg/cm² = 9.80665 N ÷ 0.0001 m² = 98,066.5 Pa. CNG at 200 kg/cm²: 200 × 98,066.5 ≈ 19.6 MPa ≈ 194 atm above outside (NIST).

03

The climb

Every step up needs a stronger wall.

  • Kitchen. A pressure cooker raises water’s boiling point to about 120 °C (see Boil).
  • Transport. CNG is compressed to 200–250 kg/cm² at stations and stored in vehicle cylinders (PNGRB).
  • Industry. Haber–Bosch, developed in 1909, makes ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen at 150–250 bar and 400–450 °C, converting under 20% per pass; about 48% of ammonia goes into urea (Smith et al. 2020).
  • Space, India. ISRO’s Gaganyaan crew module is double-walled: a pressurised metallic inner structure inside an unpressurised outer one, giving an Earth-like environment for a crew of three (ISRO).
  • The deep sea. On 23 January 1960 the bathyscaphe Trieste reached the bottom of the Challenger Deep at 35,797 ft; its crew sphere had 5-inch-thick walls (NHHC).
  • The opposite: vacuum. CERN pumps its 104 km of LHC vacuum piping down to about 10⁻¹³ atm after about two weeks of pumping (CERN).
04

The record, and how far it goes

More than the pressure at Earth’s centre, held still in a lab.

Record: in a paper published in Nature in May 2022, Dubrovinsky and colleagues used a double-stage diamond anvil cell to reach static pressures of about 900 GPa, close to a terapascal, and made new materials there (Nature).

Worked: distance from normal

900 × 10⁹ Pa ÷ 101,325 Pa ≈ 8.9 million times normal sea-level pressure. That is about 8.9 ÷ 3.6 ≈ 2.5 times the pressure in Earth’s inner core, about 3.6 million atm (National Geographic). In the other direction, the LHC’s vacuum is about 10⁻¹³ atm (CERN).

05

Try it: crush a bottle with the air

Let the atmosphere do the squeezing.

You need: an empty thin plastic water bottle with its cap, and warm tap water.

  1. Pour a little warm water into the bottle, swirl it for 30 seconds, and pour it out.
  2. Screw the cap on tightly at once.
  3. Run cold water over the bottle. As the air inside cools, its pressure falls, and the outside air crushes the bottle.
  4. Ask: which side is “pressurised” now?

Safety

Use warm tap water, not boiling water. Never heat a closed container, and never try to fill or modify a gas cylinder; cylinders must be filled and tested only by authorised stations.

06

Limits: what stops us

The higher the pressure, the more the container costs.

  • Weight. A CNG cylinder weighs about five times its gas (PNGRB); stronger materials reduce this.
  • Stored energy. Compressed gas releases its energy suddenly if a wall fails; vessels need relief valves and testing.
  • Conversion per pass. Even at 150–250 bar, Haber–Bosch converts under 20% per pass, so the gas is recycled many times (Smith et al.).
  • Tiny samples. The 900 GPa record works only on microscopic samples between diamond tips (Nature).
02 · Distance from normal

How far the records go from normal

Pressurise: from 1 atmosphere to 9 million

On a normal day the air presses at 101.325 kPa. A cooker doubles it; a CNG cylinder holds about 195 times it; the deepest ocean floor sits at about 1,080 times (our estimate); Earth’s inner core at about 3.6 million times. The 2022 diamond-anvil record reached about 8.9 million times normal. The other way, CERN’s vacuum holds about 10⁻¹³ of it.

03 · Review

Check your reasoning

Answer each one in your notebook before you open it.

Why does a CNG cylinder weigh so much more than its gas?

Its wall must resist about 200 kg/cm² on every square centimetre, so it is thick steel: about 48 kg empty for about 9 kg of gas.

How many atmospheres is 200 kg/cm²?

About 194 atmospheres above outside: 200 × 98,066.5 Pa ≈ 19.6 MPa.

Why is the urea value-per-kWh figure not a market figure?

Urea’s price is a statutory MRP below cost; the government pays the difference as subsidy.

Why does a hot, capped bottle collapse when cooled?

The air inside cools and its pressure drops below the outside air, which crushes it.
