Control · Regulate · Freeze

Why does freezing pause food instead of killing what spoils it?

Freezing takes heat out until water locks into crystals. Microbes stop growing, chemistry slows to a crawl and time nearly stops for the food. Control how fast you freeze and how cold you hold it, and you can store fish for a week or an embryo for thirty years.

Start here: the ice tray

Put a tray of water and a bowl of leftover dal in the freezer. Next morning both are solid. Thaw the dal a week later and it is still good; leave it on the counter a week and it is not. Yet the microbes in the dal are still there.

Pause and answer
  1. What does a microbe need from its surroundings to grow?
  2. What happens to the water around it when the dal freezes?
  3. Why might fast freezing keep texture better than slow freezing?
Compare your reasoning

Microbes need liquid water. When water turns to ice it is no longer available to them, so they go dormant; freezing does not kill them, and they can grow again after thawing (FSIS). Rapid freezing forms small ice crystals that do less damage to the food’s cells, so texture survives better (FSIS).

What you can do with this

  • Small business: watch the power share

    In row 1, electricity is roughly a third to a half of an ice plant’s turnover. That makes power the biggest variable cost to manage: insulation, condenser cleaning and running plants full matter more than almost anything else. Redo the sum with your own tariff and ice price.

  • Home habit: keep the freezer full and shut in a power cut

    USDA FSIS says a full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours in an outage, a half-full one for about 24, if the door stays closed (FSIS). Food kept constantly at 0 °F (−18 °C) stays safe; storage times are for quality.

  • Skill: refrigeration and cold-chain servicing

    Every row depends on compressors and seals kept in good order. The same skills serve a village ice plant, a hospital and a lab freezer. Where training is offered locally is TBC.

01

The physics in plain words

Water at 0 °C has to lose a lot more heat before it becomes ice at 0 °C.

Cooling water from 25 °C to 0 °C removes about 4.18 kJ per kg per degree (USGS). Then the temperature stops falling while the water freezes: about 334 J per gram, or 334 kJ per kg, must be removed to lock it into ice (Dickinson, Harper and Osborne, NBS). That is the latent heat of fusion.

Worked: freezing 1 kg of tap water

Cool from 25 to 0 °C: 4.18 × 25 ≈ 105 kJ. Freeze: 334 kJ. Total ≈ 439 kJ ≈ 0.12 kWh of heat. Freezing is 334 ÷ 105 ≈ 3.2 times the cooling. The Bhimavaram ice plants use 0.083–0.118 kWh of electricity per kg (row 1 above), because a refrigerator moves more heat than the electricity it uses (see Cool). Losses and sub-cooling are not counted here.

03

The climb

The same latent heat runs a fishing harbour and a space station.

  • Coastal industry. The Bhimavaram cluster in Andhra Pradesh made about 376,131 t of block ice a year, employing about 500 people; the blocks are crushed for prawns and fish in one of India’s largest aquaculture centres (BEE/SAMEEEKSHA).
  • Space. ESA’s MELFI flew to the ISS on Shuttle mission STS-121, docking on 6 July 2006. It cools about 300 litres to below −90 °C in 2 days on only 900 W, and holds it there on under 800 W. Its Brayton cooler spins at up to 96,000 rpm and gives 90 W of cooling at −97 °C, quietly enough (under 40 dB) for the crew (ESA).
  • Science. The 2017 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to cryo-electron microscopy. Jacques Dubochet found in the early 1980s how to freeze water so fast that it vitrifies, forming glass instead of crystals, so biomolecules keep their natural shape in the microscope (Nobel Prize).
  • Particle physics. CERN’s LHC magnets are held at 1.9 K (CERN), far below any freezing point except helium’s (see Cool).
  • India in space or defence. A sourced ISRO or DRDO freezing example is TBC.
04

The record, and how far it goes

Freezing can stretch time by decades.

Record: the oldest human embryo used in a successful pregnancy was at least 31 years 56 days old. It was created by IVF in May 1994, frozen in liquid nitrogen at −196 °C, and led to the birth of Thaddeus Daniel Pierce in Ohio, USA, on 26 July 2025 (Guinness World Records).

Worked: distance from normal

Earth’s normal is about 288 K. Liquid nitrogen storage is 77 K: 288 ÷ 77 ≈ 3.7 times colder in kelvin, and 288 − 77 = 211 degrees below normal. A home freezer at 255 K is only 33 degrees below normal. In time: 31 years × 365.25 + 56 ≈ 11,379 days; Guinness counts it as 31 years 56 days.

05

Try it: watch the temperature stop

See latent heat with a kitchen thermometer.

You need: a small cup of tap water, a kitchen or digital thermometer that reads below 0 °C, and a freezer.

  1. Put the thermometer in the cup and the cup in the freezer.
  2. Check every 15 minutes and note the reading.
  3. The temperature falls steadily, then sits near 0 °C for a long time while ice forms, then falls again once it is solid.
  4. The flat part is the latent heat being removed.

Safety

Never touch dry ice or liquid nitrogen without training and gloves; both cause cold burns. Use only a home freezer for this activity.

06

Limits: what stops us

Freezing is a pause button, not a cure.

  • Microbes survive. Freezing makes them dormant; once thawed, food spoils at the normal rate (FSIS).
  • Ice crystals damage cells. Slow freezing grows big crystals; that is why labs vitrify (Nobel Prize).
  • Power must stay on. A full freezer lasts about 2 days without power, a half-full one about 1 (FSIS).
  • Electricity is a large share of cost. See the value table above.
02 · Distance from normal

How far the records go from normal

Freeze: from 255 K in a kitchen to 77 K for 31 years

On a normal day Earth averages about 288 K, above water’s freezing point of 273 K. A home freezer holds food 33 degrees below normal; an ISS lab freezer holds samples below −90 °C; liquid nitrogen holds embryos at 77 K, about 3.7 times colder than normal, for more than 31 years.

03 · Review

Check your reasoning

Answer each one in your notebook before you open it.

Does freezing kill bacteria in food?

No. It makes them dormant; they can grow again after thawing.

Water in a freezer sits at 0 °C for a long time before it gets colder. Why?

The freezer is removing the latent heat of fusion, about 334 J per gram, while the water turns to ice.

Why is fast freezing better for food texture?

It forms smaller ice crystals that damage cells less.

Why can an ice plant’s electricity use be less than the heat it removes?

A refrigerator moves heat; it does not have to make it, so each kWh of electricity can carry more than 1 kWh of heat out.
