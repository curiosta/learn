Calendar · 1644 to 2025

On this day: mastering mass and energy

175 feats from Galileo’s century to today: the days people learned to convert, move, contain, regulate and direct matter and energy. One memory card per month; every entry is dated to the day by a primary or institutional source.

Open the month cards How dates were checked

Twelve memory cards

Pick a month, turn the card

Each card holds every feat in that month, sorted by day, from 1644 onwards. The coloured edge names the verb; the tag names what was being controlled. Read one card a day and ask the Socratic question: what limit did they hit, and what did they change to get past it?

  • Convert — turn one form of matter or energy into another;60
  • Move — carry mass or people across distance;56
  • Contain — hold matter, heat or pressure where it belongs;13
  • Regulate — keep a system at a set point — temperature, dose, power;25
  • Direct — steer energy or motion to an exact target;21

Method

How dates were checked

  • Exact day or nothing. An entry is here only if a primary record or an institutional source (space agency, laboratory, museum, archive, patent office, Nobel or IEEE record, encyclopaedia) gives the day. Each card links that source. Candidates with only a month or year, or where good sources disagree on the day, were left out (17 dropped).
  • Which calendar. Dates are Gregorian, as recorded where the event happened. Spaceflight dates follow the source, usually UTC; where the local date differs, the entry says so.
  • What counts. A first or a record in controlling mass and energy: heat and cold, engines and electricity, flight and speed, materials and chemistry, fusion and particle physics, energy grids, and living systems. Weapons tests appear only where they marked a first release of nuclear energy (Trinity) or a national nuclear milestone (Pokhran).
  • Verbs and objects. Convert, Move, Contain, Regulate and Direct are the course verbs; matter, heat, work/motion, fluids and living systems are the objects. Many feats fit more than one; each entry shows the main one.
  • Found a wrong date? The dataset is open as JSON (month, day, year, title, why, verb, object, source). A correction should cite a primary source.
