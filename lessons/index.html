Theme 01 · 3 lessons

How to know

See clearly. Test hard. Fail usefully. Prefer evidence over authority — and treat “I don’t know” as strength.

  1. 1

    Seeing vs believing

    Observation is not belief. Falsifiable claims take a risk; empty ones don’t. “I don’t know — here is how we’d find out” is where inquiry starts.

  2. 2

    Falsifiability

    A claim is scientific only if something could prove it wrong. Spot claims rescued by excuses or hidden in vagueness, then design a test — fixed in advance — that could kill your own favourite idea.

  3. 3

    Choosing your questions

    Every question is a road, and some lead to misery. Check a question before you answer it: is it answerable, is it testable, what does it assume, what does success optimise — and who pays if you succeed?

Theme 02 · 2 lessons

First principles

Strip problems to what is true. Rebuild from axioms. Separate nature’s limits from culture’s habits — including jobs and money.

  1. 1

    Strip it to what is true

    Judge what is possible from what a thing is made of and what physics charges — not from habit or analogy. Build an energy floor for a kettle and for aluminium, and read the gap above it as nature’s limit or ours.

  2. 2

    Frame the problem, then invent

    When machines generate ideas cheaply, what stays human is choosing the problem, framing it as a function with its physical floor, and judging the answers. Then invent on purpose with TRIZ.
